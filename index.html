Press Start and set your phone down. It reads down the page, switches tabs, opens photos, drags sliders, types, sorts lists, flips cards and answers questions. Your screen stays on until you press Pause.

The blue dot shows where it’s tapping. Works in Chrome on Android and Safari on iPhone and iPad.

Keeping the screen on

Phones dim and lock to save battery. When you press Start, this page asks the browser to keep the screen awake, and lets go when you press Pause.

Chrome, Samsung Internet, Edge and Firefox can all keep the screen on for a web page. There’s nothing to set up. It works as soon as you press Start.

Safari has supported this since iOS 16.4. Chrome and other iPhone browsers, older iOS versions and home screen apps can quietly ignore it, so on iPhone and iPad the page also plays a tiny silent video you can’t see, which keeps the screen awake the same way.

Switching to another app or tab lets the screen sleep. When you come back, the page asks again on its own. Open it from an https:// address rather than a file saved on the phone, and if the status still says it can’t keep the screen on, set Auto-Lock (iPhone) or Screen timeout (Android) to a longer time.

Questions

It opens each one so you can read along.

Why do I have to press Start?

Browsers only let a page keep the screen on after you’ve tapped something on it. That rule stops pages from draining your battery without you knowing.

Will it drain my battery?

A lit screen uses more battery than anything else here. Turn the brightness down, or plug in if it will run for hours.

Can it scroll other websites?

No. Browsers stop one page from controlling another, for your safety. To automate other sites you need a browser extension, or a tool like Playwright running on a computer.

What happens if I touch the screen?

It pauses so you can read or scroll yourself, then carries on a few seconds after you let go. You can turn that off in settings.

10 tips

  • Turn the screen brightness down to save battery
  • Plug in the charger for long sessions
  • Lock rotation so the page doesn’t flip sideways
  • Use Guided Access on iPhone to keep the screen on this page
  • Use app pinning on Android so the screen stays on the browser
  • Turn on Do Not Disturb so pop-ups don’t cover the page
  • Close other tabs to keep the browser quick
  • Keep the phone out of direct sun so it doesn’t overheat
  • Wipe the screen before putting it on display
  • Pick a slow speed for long passages of text

Making it show your content

Everything inside the <main> tags in this file is the page it drives. Replace those sections with your own text, photos, menu or script, and it will scroll through them the same way.

To have it tap or type something, add one attribute to that element:

<button data-auto="click">Next photo</button>
<input data-auto="type" data-text="pizza">
<div data-auto="tabs">…tab buttons…</div>
<div data-auto="accordion">…details…</div>
<div data-auto="carousel">…cards…</div>
<ul data-auto="check">…checkboxes…</ul>
<div data-auto="gallery">…image buttons…</div>
<div data-auto="slider" data-to="20,55">…range…</div>
<ol data-auto="sort">…items with data-order…</ol>
<div data-auto="flip">…flip cards…</div>
<fieldset data-auto="quiz" data-answer="b">…radios…</fieldset>

It acts on each one when it reaches the middle of the screen, then goes back to scrolling. To teach it something new, add a short function to the actions list in the script.

To use it on your phone, upload this one file to any free host that gives you an https:// address, such as GitHub Pages or Netlify, and open that address in your browser.

End of the page

It throws some confetti, waits a moment, goes back to the top and starts again. Turn off “Start over at the end” in settings to have it stop here instead.
